APALIT

Apalit is a municipality in Pampanga whose name is derived from the local tree species “Apalit” (Pterocarpus indicus). It is known for its long-standing blacksmith and metal industries, which date back to the Spanish period and continue today, especially in areas like Barrio Canlapan. The town is also famous for the annual fluvial festival of Saint Peter, locally known as “Apung Iru,” a colorful and lively celebration that reflects the community’s deep faith and cultural heritage.

Sulipan Bridge

The Sulipan Bridge is a historic steel bridge in Apalit, Pampanga that connects communities across the Pampanga River. Built during the American colonial period, it reflects early engineering design and has served as an important transportation link in the area. Today, it stands as both a functional structure and a reminder of the town’s historical development.

St. Peter the Apostle Parish Church

The St. Peter the Apostle Parish Church is one of the oldest and most important churches in Pampanga, established as a parish in 1597 during the Spanish colonial period. It is known for its Neo-Renaissance architecture, featuring a symmetrical façade, twin bell towers, and beautifully painted interiors. The church is also famous for the “Apung Iru” (St. Peter) devotion and the annual Libad fluvial procession, where the image of St. Peter is paraded along the river—a major religious event in Pampanga. A unique highlight is that the church houses a relic of Saint Peter, making it one of the very few churches in the world (outside Rome) with such a sacred relic.

La Sulipena

La Sulipena is a historic ancestral residence located in Barangay Sulipan, Apalit, Pampanga. It was once a grand bahay na bato owned by the Arnedo family, a prominent and wealthy clan during the Spanish and American periods. The estate actually consisted of two large houses used for family living and entertaining important guests. La Sulipena became famous as a venue for elegant gatherings attended by high-ranking officials and even foreign dignitaries, making it a symbol of wealth, influence, and high society in Pampanga during the 19th century. Today, it is remembered as a cultural and historical landmark that reflects the rich heritage and aristocratic lifestyle of old Pampanga.

Ing Bale ning Apung Iru

Ing Bale ning Apung Iru (The House of Apung Iru) is a heritage house and small museum located near the St. Peter Parish Church in Apalit, Pampanga. It is dedicated to the devotion to Apung Iru (Saint Peter) and preserves religious artifacts, old images, and historical items related to the famous Libad fluvial procession. The house serves as a cultural and spiritual center, showcasing the deep faith and traditions of the people of Apalit.
