BACOLOR

Bacolor is a historic town in Pampanga known for its rich past and strong resilience. It is one of the oldest settlements in the province, dating back to the 1500s, and once served as the capital of Pampanga for over 200 years. The town also played an important role in Philippine history, even becoming the temporary capital of the Philippines during the British occupation (1762–1764). Bacolor is widely known today for its survival after the eruption of Mount Pinatubo, where lahar buried much of the town—yet its people rebuilt and preserved their heritage, especially landmarks like the partially buried San Guillermo Church.

San Guillermo Parish Church

The San Guillermo Parish Church is a historic church in Bacolor, Pampanga, famous for being partially buried by lahar during the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo. Originally built in 1576, the church was covered by volcanic mudflows, leaving only the upper portion visible today. Because of this, visitors now enter through what used to be the windows, and the floor is much lower than ground level. Despite the disaster, the church was preserved and continues to function, becoming a symbol of faith, resilience, and survival for the people of Bacolor.

Museo de Bacolor

The Museo de Bacolor, also known as Recuerdos Sagrados de Bacolor (Sacred Memorabilia of Bacolor), is a heritage museum located inside the convent of the half-buried San Guillermo Parish Church in Bacolor, Pampanga. It showcases religious artifacts, old photographs, and historical items that reflect the town’s rich heritage and resilience following the 1991 Mount Pinatubo eruption.

Archdiocesan Shrine of Our Lady of Lourdes of Cabetican

The Archdiocesan Shrine of Our Lady of Lourdes of Cabetican is a modern Catholic church and pilgrimage site located in Bacolor, Pampanga. It was built after the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo, when many residents were relocated to Barangay Cabetican. The shrine became a center of faith and hope for the community as they rebuilt their lives. The church is dedicated to Our Lady of Lourdes and is known for its peaceful atmosphere, grotto, and regular religious activities, attracting devotees and visitors from different areas.

Simon De Anda Monument

The Simon de Anda Monument is a historical landmark located within the town hall grounds of Bacolor, Pampanga. It honors Simon de Anda y Salazar, who moved to Bacolor during the British invasion of Manila and established it as the capital from 1762 to 1764. The monument stands as a tribute to his legacy and the town’s important role in Philippine history.
